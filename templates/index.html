Epidemiological Takeaways

WHO Data Findings
1
HIV/AIDS Pediatric Mortality (~59.7% Influence)

During 2000–2015, nations suffering untreated HIV epidemics experienced dramatic plunges in life expectancy down into the 40s and 50s. Antiretroviral therapy and prevention of mother-to-child transmission yield the highest marginal longevity dividend of any factor.

2
Income Composition & HDI (~17.1% Influence)

Socioeconomic development, clean water, housing, and food security create the resilient base that protects populations from communicable diseases and premature death.

3
Adult Mortality Probability (~12.7% Influence)

Premature mortality between ages 15 and 60 (driven by trauma, cardiovascular disease, and non-communicable illnesses) accounts for the primary divergence in lifespans between upper-middle and developed economies.

4
Schooling & Immunization Synergies

Universal vaccination (Polio, Measles, DTP3) paired with maternal education above 12 years creates an exponential compounding effect on child survival rates.
